Briefing for the Radiologist Readiness Survey

KPJ Radiology Centre of Reference (CoR)

One coordinated radiology network across KPJ, with access to subspecialty expertise throughout the network.

A 5-minute read before the Radiologist Readiness Survey.

1 · The need

Why a CoR?

Subspecialty expertise is limited. Only 17 of the 69 resident radiologists have a recorded subspecialty, with thin coverage of most subspecialties.

Complex cases rely on who you know. Referring doctors phone named specialists; some specialised reporting goes outside KPJ.

Practice varies between sites. Protocols differ, and there is no network route for second opinions or peer review.

Radiologists across 29 KPJ hospitals, by appointment type, from hospital consultant rosters (radiology mapping report, 17 Oct 2026; for validation).

The images travel through the enterprise PACS; the patient moves only if a procedure needs it.

Routine work stays local. Only cases needing higher-level expertise, a specialised procedure or an independent review are referred.

Use subspecialty skills better

Pool complex cases so subspecialists work more in their field.

Consistent standards

Standard KPJ imaging protocols for each subspecialty, adaptable to local equipment, with templates and peer review, set by radiologists.

Training and research

Fellowships, mentoring and a shared case registry.

Led clinically through a Steering Committee, Regional CoR Leads and subspecialty groups (IR, MSK, Neuro, Cardiac, Breast). Regional CoR Leads are confirmed for the Southern, Northern and East Coast, Central and East Malaysia regions; a lead for each subspecialty group is still to be appointed. CoR headquarters and primary IR hub: KPJ Tawakkal. Phase 1 is a limited pilot targeted from January 2027, starting with co-reporting and specialised procedures, for internal KPJ referrals only.

In the survey: Section C asks whether you would receive CoR referrals in your subspecialty.

3 · Services

Three CoR services, two in Phase 1

Specialised Procedures

Advanced imaging or image-guided intervention needing CoR-level skills or equipment. The IR radiologist travels, or the patient transfers.

Co-Reporting

You report first; a CoR subspecialist reviews and amends; you approve. One final report, with one fee to the patient. How the report is signed off is still to be decided.

Second-Opinion Reporting

Later phase, not in Phase 1. Deferred pending legal review and radiologists' feedback. An independent subspecialist report on a study already reported. A separate report with patient consent and a separate fee; both reports stay in the record.

Co-reporting is also a learning route for general radiologists and a source of support for difficult cases. It will start on a voluntary basis, framed as an improvement target. Which hospitals start, and what target share of cases suits each region, are still being worked out.

In the survey: Section C asks about co-reporting, a reasonable target share, report sign-off, imaging protocols and mentoring. Section E asks for your views on second opinions, to help plan the later phase.

4 · In practice

How it would work for you

Referral
Doctors refer through the Remedi e-referral platform. A CoR Coordinator triages and assigns each case.
Image access
Through the enterprise PACS, limited to the referred study and based on patient consent.
Responsibility
Where both radiologists sign the report, they are equally liable, whatever the fee split. How co-reports are signed off is still to be decided (see Open questions). The consent form will be updated to cover co-reporting.
Second opinions
Not part of Phase 1. Proposals so far: requests only through the treating KPJ clinician with patient consent, the primary radiologist is notified, and the second reader can see the original report. These are being worked through with Legal.
Turnaround
CoR cases: 4 hours if urgent, 24 hours if routine, tracked on a dashboard. Group reporting turnaround times will be issued as a clinical guideline, not a policy, and will cover how critical results are communicated.
Remote reporting
A KPJ standard (devices, connectivity, security) is being built on the College teleradiology guideline.
Practising across KPJ
You need credentialing at each hospital. Listing all KPJ hospitals in your region on your 2027 APC is advised.

In the survey: Sections E and F ask about the protections you would need and your remote-reporting setup. Section B asks how you would like to be briefed on the CoR. A call for volunteers to test the platform will follow the survey.
